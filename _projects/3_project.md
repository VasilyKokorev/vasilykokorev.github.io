Before JWST, the first few hundred million years of cosmic history were essentially a blank page. They are not blank any more — but what has been written there is stranger than anyone expected. Galaxies at z &gt; 10 are more numerous and more UV-luminous than pre-launch models allowed, and the explanations on offer range from bursty star formation, to top-heavy initial mass functions, to almost dust-free lines of sight.

My work here is mostly spectroscopic: taking the candidates that imaging surveys throw up and either confirming them or killing them, then using NIRSpec to measure what those galaxies are actually made of — their stellar populations, their ionized gas, and how quickly they turn gas into stars. I work on this through **CAPERS** in the blank fields and **GLIMPSE**, the deepest JWST imaging of a lensing cluster, at the faint end.

---

### [CAPERS: Two UV-bright Galaxies at z > 10](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...988L..10K/abstract)

*Kokorev et al. (2025), ApJL*

The first results out of CAPERS, using just 14% of the planned data: two spectroscopic confirmations at z = 10.562 and z = 11.013 in PRIMER-UDS. One of them shows strong Hγ (EW<sub>0</sub> ~ 70 Å), which is a direct fingerprint of a recent burst. Putting these together with everything else confirmed at z &gt; 10, most bright galaxies at these redshifts appear to be caught mid-burst, with a median SFR<sub>10</sub>/SFR<sub>100</sub> ~ 2 — a real, if partial, answer to why the bright end of the UV luminosity function is so overpopulated.

### [A Glimpse of the New Redshift Frontier through AS1063](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2025ApJ...983L..22K/abstract)

*Kokorev et al. (2025), ApJL*

Two galaxy candidates at 15.7 &lt; z &lt; 16.4, found in the ultra-deep GLIMPSE imaging of Abell S1063. Lensing by the cluster pushes us down to M<sub>UV</sub> = −17, roughly two magnitudes fainter than blank-field surveys reach at these redshifts, and both objects sit right at that limit with very blue slopes (β ≃ −2.87). Their number density is in clear tension with pre-JWST predictions, extending the galaxy over-abundance from z ~ 10 out to z ~ 17, and their star formation histories make them plausible progenitors of the UV-bright systems JWST now routinely finds at z = 10–14.
